Who we are

A small club in Limassol, plugged into 1.4 million people.

Limassol Fraternité is part of Lions Clubs International — the largest service organisation on earth, active in over 200 countries since 1917. That gives a club of our size something unusual: local hands, global backing.

It started when a Chicago businessman named Melvin Jones told his business club that it should reach beyond its own interests and take responsibility for the community around it. That was 1917. The motto came later, chosen by a worldwide vote of members in 1954, and it is still only two words long: We Serve.

Our members come from Belgian, Canadian, Cypriot, French and Italian backgrounds. Meetings run in English, and drift into Greek and French when the argument gets interesting. We sit in District 117-B, covering Northern Greece and Cyprus.

What we are not is a dinner club that writes cheques. Every project we run has a named owner, a number attached to it, and a date it has to be finished by.

The difference

Charity hands out. Development builds capacity.
We are moving to the second thing.

Handing someone a meal solves tonight. Handing them a system solves the year. Every project we take on is judged against four questions.

01

Does it measure?

If we cannot count it, we cannot improve it. Kilograms of waste, screenings delivered, meals routed — every project produces a number we publish, including the disappointing ones.

02

Does it outlast us?

We prefer building a process a community can run without us over an event they need us to repeat. Success looks like being made unnecessary.

03

Does it use the tools of now?

Shared logistics boards, open datasets, digital literacy programmes. Technology is not a gimmick bolted on the end — it is often the cheapest way to multiply a small team.

04

Does it grow a leader?

Every project is owned by a member, not a committee. People join to help and leave able to run things. That is the quiet output nobody puts on a poster.

What you plug into

Joining a club this small opens doors this big.

These are real Lions International programmes, open to members of any club — including ours. Ask us about any of them.

Ages 12–30

Leo clubs

The youth arm of Lions International. Alpha Leos are 12–18, Omega Leos 18–30. Leos run their own service projects and build leadership experience that transfers straight onto a CV.

Ages 15–22

Youth Camps & Exchange

Live abroad with a host Lions family, join their service projects and meet young people from dozens of countries. No academic requirement and no tuition fee — this is cultural exchange, not a study programme.

Ages 11–13

Peace Poster Contest

Run for nearly four decades. Clubs sponsor it in local schools and youth groups; entrants express what peace means to them through art. The international grand prize is US$5,000, with 23 merit awards of US$500.

Schools

Lions Quest

A social-emotional learning programme delivered in schools worldwide and funded through LCIF grants — covering resilience, decision-making and drug-prevention education for young people.

Funding

LCIF grants

Lions Clubs International Foundation has awarded more than US$1.3 billion in grants across 200 countries. Club and district community grants exist precisely so a small club can fund a good idea. If you bring us one, there is a route to paying for it.

By 1 July 2027

Mission 1.5

Lions International is working to reach 1.5 million members worldwide by July 2027, so that clubs can serve more people than ever. That is the honest reason we are recruiting — and why a new member matters here.
